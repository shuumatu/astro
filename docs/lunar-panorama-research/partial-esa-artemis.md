## 0. BOTTOM LINE (read this first)

**No genuine ESA lunar SURFACE panorama exists. None has ever existed.**

- ESA has **never landed anything on the Moon**. ESA's own words: Argonaut is *"ESA's first lunar
  lander"*, with the Lunar Descent Element delivered *"in 2030 for the first operational mission,
  ArgoNET, expected in 2031."*
- ESA's only lunar mission to date, **SMART-1 (2003–2006), was an ORBITER** and ended in a planned
  **impact**. It produced orbital imagery, never surface imagery.
- Every ESA-owned picture of a *lunar surface* that this research could reach is an **artist's
  impression / illustration** credited to ESA's graphics studio (ESA–ATG / ATG Medialab), explicitly
  captioned *"Artist's impression…"*. These are **CGI, not photographs.**

**No genuine Artemis-era crewed lunar SURFACE panorama exists either.**

- **Artemis I (2022) was uncrewed and never landed** — cameras on Orion's *solar arrays* photographed
  the Moon *from distant retrograde orbit*.
- **Artemis II FLEW and splashed down 2026-04-10** (this is new since most reference material). It was
  crewed but was a **free-return flyby — it never landed**. It returned **real** lunar photographs, but
  they were shot **through Orion's windows / by Orion's cameras in flight**, not from the surface.
- **Artemis III has been restructured and will NOT land.** NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman announced
  on **Friday, 27 February 2026** that the Artemis III landing was removed; Artemis III becomes a
  low-Earth-orbit technology/rendezvous flight (~mid-2027) and **Artemis IV (~2028) becomes the first
  crewed landing**. So as of 2026-09-12 the Artemis programme has put **zero** humans on the surface.
- NASA's Artemis III "surface" imagery in the official image library is titled **"Artist's Concepts…"**
  and carries `AVAIL:Photographer = "Photo courtesy SpaceX"`. The Artemis III landing-region visuals are
  a **visualization rendered from Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data**. Both are CGI/derived — not
  surface photography.
- **Real** lunar surface imagery in the Artemis era does exist, but it is from **uncrewed commercial
  CLPS landers** (Intuitive Machines IM-1 *Odysseus*, Feb 2024; Firefly *Blue Ghost* Mission 1, Mar
  2025). These are NASA-funded *Artemis-campaign* deliveries, but they are **not "Artemis" missions**,
  are not crewed, and no official *stitched surface panorama* from them was verified in this session.

**Licence positions**

- **ESA:** images on the ESA Website are **copyright ESA / third parties**, free for
  **educational, editorial and/or informational** use **only**, with mandatory credit `©ESA`.
  **Commercial use is prohibited** without separate written authorisation. **Some** (not all) ESA
  images are additionally offered under **CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO**. ESA's own images contradict any blanket
  "ESA = CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO" assumption: verified examples below carry **"ESA Standard Licence"**,

### 1.2 Verbatim mission text — SMART-1 is an orbiter

From **https://sci.esa.int/web/smart-1/31407-summary** (page title `ESA Science & Technology - Summary`):

> "It is the first time that Europe has sent a spacecraft to the Moon. In addition to the use of solar
> electric primary propulsion to reach Earth's natural satellite, the spacecraft carries out a complete
> programme of scientific observations **in lunar orbit**."

> "SMART-1 was launched succesfully as an Ariane-5 auxiliary payload on 27 September 2003 and **entered
> lunar orbit on 15 November 2004**. The planetary objective selected for the SMART-1 mission was to
> **orbit the Moon** for a nominal period of 6 months, with a possible extension of one year."

> "In the second period, high-resolution coverage of the Moon on the equator and part of the northern
> hemisphere took place due to the favourable illumination conditions."

From **https://sci.esa.int/web/smart-1** (mission summary / meta description):

> "SMART-1 was used to test solar electric propulsion and other deep-space technologies, while
> performing scientific observations of the Moon. … The mission ended on 3 September 2006 when the
> spacecraft, in a planned manoeuvre, **impacted the lunar surface** in the Lacus Excellentiae region."

**Note on the end of mission:** SMART-1 *impacted* the surface as a planned end-of-life manoeuvre. An
impact is not a landing and cannot produce surface imagery. Do not describe SMART-1 as having "landed"
or "touched down".

## 4. Artemis I / II / III — reality check

### 4.1 Artemis I (2022): uncrewed, never landed, no surface imagery

Evidence from the official NASA Image and Video Library API (`https://images-api.nasa.gov/search`),
querying item descriptions (verbatim):

- `nasa_id=art001e000669` (2022-11-27) —
  > "art001e000669 (Nov. 27, 2022) On flight day 12 of the 25.5-day Artemis I mission, a **camera on the
  > tip of one of Orion's solar arrays** captured the Moon as Orion **travels in distant retrograde
  > orbit** around the Moon."

- `nasa_id=art001e001819` (2022-12-01) —
  > "art001e001819 (Dec. 1, 2022) On flight day 16, **a camera mounted on one of Orion's solar arrays**
  > snapped this image of our Moon as the spacecraft **prepared to exit distant retrograde orbit**
  > during the Artemis I mission."

Day-10 items confirm the **Distant Retrograde Orbit Insertion (DRI) burn** (`nasa_id="Artemis I MCC
DRI"`, 2022-11-25). Artemis I carried **no crew** and had **no lander**; the "selfie"/Earth-and-Moon
images are from Orion's solar-array cameras **in space**. **No Artemis I lunar surface imagery exists.**

### 4.2 Artemis II: FLEW, crewed, splashed down 2026-04-10 — but it did NOT land

**This is the most important currency correction in this report.** At the session date of 2026-09-12,
Artemis II has **already flown**.

- `nasa_id=KSC-20260410-PH-JNV01_0001` (2026-04-10) —

- `nasa_id=KSC-20260410-PH-JMB01_0003` (2026-04-10) —

So the Artemis II crew was **Wiseman (CDR), Glover (Pilot), Koch (MS, NASA), Hansen (MS, CSA)**.

### 8.4 The ESA multimedia search is JavaScript-only — not machine-searchable

`https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Search?q=panorama` returns **200 / text/html / 61391 bytes**, and
`https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Search?q=lunar+lander` returns
`206 | text/html | 4096 | 0a0a09090a090a3c21646f63`. Extracting images/anchors from the saved HTML
yields **zero result rows** (only nav/chrome; e.g. the strings `Images`, `Photo Library for
Professionals`). Results are injected client-side.

**Impact — the key limitation on Part A's exhaustiveness:** it was **not possible to run an exhaustive,
authoritative query of ESA's own image archive** for the term "panorama" or for lunar surface material.
The ESA negative in §2 therefore rests on (a) ESA's explicit *"ESA's first lunar lander"* statements,
(b) SMART-1's documented orbital-only mission profile, and (c) manual inspection of the specific ESA
image pages reachable by other means — **not** on a complete archive enumeration. Any future work should
either drive a headless browser against the ESA search UI or find its JSON endpoint.

### 8.5 Bing search is degraded as an index

`www.bing.com/search?q=...` returns **200**, but:
- `site:` operators were **not honoured** — e.g. the query

  Britannica, `esahubble.org`, `theesa.com`, `esa.org`, `gov.uk` — i.e. it disregarded the `site:`
  restriction entirely.
- Results are served as a Chinese-market landing page; the raw HTML contains no usable result rows
  (result extraction returned only MSN/ICP-filing boilerplate).
- `web_search` was therefore the effective discovery tool for URLs; **all discovered URLs were then
  independently verified by proxy probe/GET before being cited**, per the task's requirement.
